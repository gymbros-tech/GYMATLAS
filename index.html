EXERCISES FOR CHEST
01
PUSH
Chest · Shoulders · Triceps
Push-Ups
Primary: Chest · Triceps · Anterior Deltoid
The foundational bodyweight pressing movement. Lower your chest to the floor under control, then push explosively. Variations: wide, narrow, diamond, decline, pike, and archer push-ups.
3–5 Sets 10–25 Reps Bodyweight Compound
Difficulty
Bench Press
Primary: Pectoralis Major · Secondary: Triceps, Deltoid
King of chest exercises. Lie on bench, lower barbell to mid-chest with elbows at ~45°, press explosively. Flat targets mid-chest, incline hits upper chest, decline emphasizes lower fibers.
4 Sets 6–12 Reps Barbell Compound
Difficulty
Dumbbell Press
Primary: Pectoralis Major · Triceps · Anterior Deltoid
Greater range of motion than barbell. Each arm works independently, preventing strength imbalances. Allows natural wrist rotation for joint-friendly pressing. Excellent for chest development and symmetry.
3–4 Sets 8–15 Reps Dumbbell Compound
Difficulty
Chest Fly
Primary: Pectoralis Major · Secondary: Anterior Deltoid
Pure chest isolation. Arc the arms wide to stretch the chest fully, then squeeze together at the top. Cable version provides constant tension throughout the movement for superior hypertrophy stimulus.
3 Sets 12–15 Reps Isolation Dumbbell/Cable
Difficulty
Shoulder Press (OHP)
Primary: Deltoids · Secondary: Triceps, Upper Traps
The overhead press is the definitive shoulder builder. Press bar or dumbbells from shoulder height to full lockout overhead. Engages all three deltoid heads and builds impressive shoulder width.
4 Sets 6–12 Reps Barbell/DB Compound
Difficulty
Lateral Raises
Primary: Lateral (Medial) Deltoid · Secondary: Supraspinatus
The key exercise for building shoulder width. Raise dumbbells to shoulder height with a slight forward lean and slight elbow bend. Lead with elbows, keep a controlled descent. Essential for that 3D shoulder look.
3–4 Sets 12–20 Reps Dumbbell Isolation
Difficulty
Dips
Primary: Triceps, Lower Chest · Secondary: Anterior Deltoid
Compound bodyweight push. Upright torso targets triceps more; leaning forward shifts load to lower chest. Lower until shoulders are below elbows, then press up. Add weight via belt for progressive overload.
3–4 Sets 8–15 Reps Bodyweight Compound
Difficulty
Triceps Pushdown
Primary: Triceps Brachii (all 3 heads)
Cable machine staple for arm development. Keep elbows pinned to sides, extend forearms down until lockout, squeeze hard. Use rope attachment for outer head emphasis or straight bar for overall mass.
3 Sets 12–15 Reps Cable Isolation
Difficulty
02
PULL
Back · Biceps · Rear Delts
Pull-Ups / Chin-Ups
Primary: Latissimus Dorsi · Biceps · Rear Deltoid
The ultimate upper body pulling test. Wide grip overhand (pull-up) hits lats harder; underhand narrow grip (chin-up) emphasizes biceps. Pull chest to bar, squeeze shoulder blades at top.
4 Sets 6–15 Reps Bodyweight Compound
Difficulty
Lat Pulldown
Primary: Latissimus Dorsi · Secondary: Biceps, Teres Major
Cable machine pull-up alternative. Grip wide, pull bar to upper chest while driving elbows down and back. Lean slightly back, arch chest upward. Excellent for building lat width and learning the pull-up movement pattern.
3–4 Sets 10–12 Reps Cable Machine Compound
Difficulty
Barbell Row
Primary: Lats, Mid-Traps, Rhomboids · Secondary: Biceps, Rear Delts
Bent-over barbell row builds serious back thickness. Hinge at hips ~45°, pull bar to lower chest/upper abs, squeeze shoulder blades. Overhand grip hits traps; underhand grip emphasizes lower lats and biceps.
4 Sets 6–10 Reps Barbell Compound
Difficulty
Bicep Curls
Primary: Biceps Brachii · Secondary: Brachialis, Brachioradialis
The quintessential arm exercise. Supinate wrist as you curl, squeeze hard at the top, control the descent. Barbell allows heavier loads; dumbbell allows rotation. Alternate or simultaneous — both work well for bicep peak and size.
3 Sets 10–15 Reps Barbell/DB Isolation
Difficulty
Face Pulls
Primary: Rear Deltoids · Rotator Cuff · Upper Traps
Essential for shoulder health and posterior delt development. Pull rope to face level with elbows high and wide, externally rotating at peak. Vital for balancing all the pressing work. Do these often.
3 Sets 15–20 Reps Cable Machine Isolation
Difficulty
Deadlifts
Primary: Hamstrings, Glutes, Erectors · Secondary: Lats, Traps, Quads
The king of all exercises — works nearly every muscle in the body. Hinge at hips, flat back, bar over mid-foot. Drive through the floor, keep bar close to shins, lock out hips at top.
4 Sets 3–8 Reps Barbell Compound
Difficulty
03
LEGS
Quads · Hamstrings · Glutes · Calves
Squats
Primary: Quadriceps, Glutes · Secondary: Hamstrings, Core, Erectors
The foundational lower body movement. Bar on traps, feet shoulder-width, squat to parallel or below. Drive knees out, keep chest up. Back squat builds mass; front squat hits quads harder.
4–5 Sets 5–12 Reps Barbell Compound
Difficulty
Lunges
Primary: Quadriceps, Glutes · Secondary: Hamstrings, Calves, Core
Unilateral leg exercise that builds balance, coordination, and corrects imbalances. Step forward, lower back knee toward floor, push off front heel to return. Walking lunges, reverse lunges, and Bulgarian split squats are valuable variations.
3 Sets 10–15 Reps/leg Bodyweight/DB Compound
Difficulty
Romanian Deadlift
Primary: Hamstrings, Glutes · Secondary: Lower Back, Calves
Hinge-dominant movement for hamstring and glute development. Start standing, push hips back while lowering bar along shins, feel the hamstring stretch, then drive hips forward to return.
3–4 Sets 8–12 Reps Barbell/DB Compound
Difficulty
Hip Thrust
Primary: Gluteus Maximus · Secondary: Hamstrings, Core
The most effective exercise for glute development. Upper back on bench, barbell across hips — drive hips up by squeezing glutes hard, hold for 1 second at the top. Research consistently shows this activates glutes more than any other movement.
4 Sets 10–15 Reps Barbell/DB Compound
Difficulty
Calf Raises
Primary: Gastrocnemius · Secondary: Soleus, Tibialis
Stand on edge of step for full range of motion — deep stretch at bottom, full contraction at top. High rep ranges work well (15–30). Seated calf raises (bent knee) target the deeper soleus muscle.
4 Sets 15–25 Reps Machine/BW Isolation
Difficulty
04
CORE
Abs · Obliques · Lower Back
Plank
Primary: Transverse Abdominis · Rectus Abdominis · Obliques
The gold standard isometric core exercise. Hold a perfect straight line from head to heels, engaging every core muscle simultaneously. Progress by adding time, weight plates on back, or moving to side planks and RKC planks.
3–5 Sets 30–90 Sec Bodyweight Isometric
Difficulty
Crunches / Sit-Ups
Primary: Rectus Abdominis · Secondary: Hip Flexors, Obliques
Classic ab isolation. Focus on curling the torso, not pulling the neck. Exhale at the top, squeeze abs hard, lower under control. Weighted crunches, cable crunches, and decline sit-ups add progressive overload.
3–4 Sets 15–25 Reps Bodyweight Isolation
Difficulty
Hanging Leg Raises
Primary: Lower Abs (Rectus Abdominis) · Hip Flexors
Hang from a bar and raise legs to parallel or above. The hardest ab movement. Avoid swinging — control every inch. Bent knee version is easier; straight legs are advanced. Tremendous lower ab and hip flexor strength builder.
3 Sets 10–15 Reps Bodyweight Compound
Difficulty
Russian Twists
Primary: Obliques · Secondary: Rectus Abdominis, Hip Flexors
Seated rotational exercise targeting the obliques. Feet elevated (harder) or on ground, rotate torso side to side touching the floor. Add a weight plate or medicine ball for resistance. Great for rotational power and waist definition.
3 Sets 20–30 Reps Bodyweight/DB Isolation
Difficulty
05
FULL BODY
Power · Athletic · Total
Burpees
Full Body · Conditioning · Cardiovascular
The ultimate full-body conditioning exercise. From standing, drop to push-up position, perform a push-up, jump feet to hands, then explode up with a jump and clap overhead. Extremely demanding — burns massive calories and builds total body endurance.
3–5 Sets 10–20 Reps Bodyweight Compound
Difficulty
Clean and Press
Full Body: Legs, Back, Shoulders, Core, Arms
Olympic-style compound movement that develops explosive power from head to toe. Clean the bar from the floor to shoulder height, then press overhead. Develops athletic power, coordination, and total body strength simultaneously.
4 Sets 4–6 Reps Barbell Power
Difficulty
Kettlebell Swings
Primary: Glutes, Hamstrings · Secondary: Core, Back, Shoulders
Hip-hinge power movement that builds explosive posterior chain strength. Drive hips forward to swing the kettlebell, not arms. At peak, body is straight and glutes fully contracted. Excellent for fat loss, conditioning, and athletic power.
3–5 Sets 15–20 Reps Kettlebell Power
Difficulty
06
CARDIO
Endurance · Fat Loss · Heart Health
Running / Jogging
Cardiovascular System · Legs · Core Stability
The most accessible cardio. Start with 20–30 minute easy runs, build up over weeks. LISS (low intensity steady state) burns fat effectively; HIIT intervals build cardiovascular capacity faster. Aim for 150+ minutes of moderate cardio per week.
20–60 Min 3–5x/week Treadmill/Road LISS/HIIT
Difficulty
Jump Rope
Cardiovascular · Calves · Coordination · Core
Pound for pound the best cardio tool. Burns up to 1000 cal/hour, improves coordination and rhythm, builds calf strength. Start with 30-second intervals. Progress to double-unders. 10 minutes of jumping rope ≈ 30 minutes of jogging.
10–30 Min Intervals Jump Rope HIIT
Difficulty
Cycling / Spin Bike
Cardiovascular · Quads · Glutes · Hamstrings
Low-impact cardio excellent for knee health while still torching calories and building leg endurance. Stationary spin classes are high intensity; road cycling builds sustained aerobic capacity. Adjust resistance for LISS or HIIT training.
20–60 Min 3–5x/week Bike/Machine LISS
Difficulty
07
ISOLATION
Precision · Detail · Refinement
Cable Crossover
Primary: Pectoralis Major (inner chest emphasis)
Cables provide constant tension through the full arc, unlike dumbbells that lose resistance at peak contraction. High cables target lower chest; low cables target upper chest. Unmatched for chest definition and inner chest development.
3 Sets 12–15 Reps Cable Station Isolation
Difficulty
Pec Deck / Chest Fly Machine
Primary: Pectoralis Major · Sternal Head
Guided machine for chest isolation. Sit upright, arms on pads, squeeze pads together focusing on the center of the chest. Ideal for warming up, finisher sets, or when training without a spotter. Excellent pump for chest development.
3 Sets 12–15 Reps Machine Isolation
Difficulty
Skull Crushers
Primary: Long Head Triceps · Secondary: Lateral/Medial Head
Lying triceps extension with EZ-bar or dumbbells. Lower bar to forehead by bending only at the elbows. Excellent for targeting the long head of the triceps for arm thickness. Use controlled tempo throughout.
3 Sets 10–12 Reps EZ-Bar/DB Isolation
Difficulty
Hammer Curls
Primary: Brachialis · Secondary: Brachioradialis, Biceps
Neutral grip curl that develops the brachialis — the muscle that pushes the bicep peak up. Essential for arm thickness and forearm development. The neutral wrist position also reduces strain on the elbow joint.
3 Sets 12–15 Reps Dumbbell Isolation
Difficulty
Leg Extension
Primary: Quadriceps (all 4 heads) · Focus: Rectus Femoris
Isolation machine for quad definition and strength. Seat adjusted so knees align with machine pivot, extend to lockout squeezing quads hard. Best used at end of leg session. Moderate weight, high reps, and slow tempo work excellently.
3 Sets 12–15 Reps Machine Isolation
Difficulty
